# Robert C. Martin's SRP and component (package) principles: formulations, evolution, metrics, critiques

Labels: [primary] = read in the original text (PDF/blog/slides extracted in this session); [secondary] = taken from a summary, abstract, search excerpt or reader highlight. Evidence type is given as (opinion/experience), (definition/argument) or (empirical: setting, n). Quotes keep original typos. Clean Architecture (2017) could not be opened directly (O'Reilly returned 403), so its quotes come from verbatim Kindle highlights posted on Goodreads and from a Habr article, cross-checked where possible; they are marked [secondary].

## 1. SRP formulations and their evolution; what "reason to change" means; rejected readings

### Takeaway
The substance has stayed the same: cohesion around a single source of change. What changed is the unit and the operational test. The unit went from "class" (2002/2003) to "subsystem, module, class, or even a function" (2010), then "software module" (2014), then "module" plus the component and boundary levels (2017). The test went from "a motive/axis of change" (and only if that change actually occurs) to "people" (2014) to "one, and only one, actor" (2017). Martin explicitly rejects "every module should do just one thing" as SRP and assigns "do one thing" to functions.

### Cited Findings
- **Lineage, in Martin's own account.** He cites Parnas (1972), Dijkstra's separation of concerns, and coupling/cohesion as introduced by Constantine and "amplified by Tom DeMarco, Meilir Page-Jones and many others". He writes: "In the late 1990s I tried to consolidate these notions into a principle, which I called: The Single Responsibility Principle. (I have this vague feeling that I stole the name of this principle from Bertrand Meyer, but I have not been able to confirm that.)" [primary; historical self-account] — [Martin 2014, "The Single Responsibility Principle", Clean Coder Blog, 8 May 2014](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/08/SingleReponsibilityPrinciple.html)
- **SRP is absent from the 1996–97 C++ Report "Principles" series.** The "Reprise" in the sixth column lists "8 principles": OCP (Jan 1996), LSP (Mar 1996), DIP (Jun 1996), ISP (Aug 1996), plus REP, CRP, CCP and ADP from "Granularity"; SDP and SAP follow in that column. SRP is also absent from the 2000 Object Mentor paper. [primary] — [Martin 1997, "Stability", C++ Report (Engineering Notebook column 6), archived Object Mentor PDF](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf); [Martin 2000, "Design Principles and Design Patterns", Object Mentor](http://staff.cs.utu.fi/~jounsmed/doos_06/material/DesignPrinciplesAndPatterns.pdf)

- **2010.** "Gather together those things that change for the same reason, and separate those things that change for different reasons. ... it says that a subsystem, module, class, or even a function, should not have more than one reason to change." - **2017: SRP scaled up.** "As stated earlier, the CCP is the component form of the SRP." "The SRP tells us where to draw our boundaries." "Boundaries are drawn where there is an axis of change." [secondary: highlights] — [Goodreads highlights](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/29536501-osvaldo-santana-neto)
- **2020 restatement:** "Gather together the things that change for the same reasons. Separate things that change for different reasons." "We make sure that modules that change for different reasons do not have dependencies that tangle them." [primary] — [Martin 2020, "Solid Relevance", Clean Coder Blog, 18 Oct 2020](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2020/10/18/Solid-Relevance.html)

### Inferences
- **Draft to book.** The change from "Almost certainly they should" to "That depends upon how the application is changing" softened SRP from a rule into a reactive one: separate when the symptom appears. The 2014 and 2017 actor framing moves the test from observed change history to predicted sources of change in the organization.
- **Why it changed.** Martin's own stated reason is confusion over "What defines a reason to change?" (bug fixes, refactorings) and the "do one thing" misreading. Each version is supported only by argument and examples (expert opinion). No version offers empirical validation.
- **What "do one thing" means in the rejection.** The 2017 rejection is about the criterion (size or "one thing" versus one source of change), not the unit. The 2010 text applied the reason-to-change test "even [to] a function".
- **Two senses of "responsible".** In "Stability" and the 2000 paper, a package with dependents is "responsible to those three packages", meaning depended upon. In SRP 2014/2017, a module is "responsible to" people or actors. The two senses differ.

### Gaps
- The 2003 Java edition text was not read directly. Its principle wording and the "depends upon how the application is changing" passage were verified only in the 2006 C# edition (Cecconi 2014 quotes the same passage).
- Not verified verbatim from Clean Architecture: the definition of "module" (recalled as "source file", or else "a cohesive set of functions and data structures") and the ch. 7 conclusion (SRP reappears as CCP at component level and as the "Axis of Change" at architecture level).
- The Meyer origin of the name remains unconfirmed (Martin says so himself). The draft's date is unknown.
- Not checked: whether Martin's post-2020 books (e.g., *Functional Design*, 2023) restate SRP.

## 2. Component principles (REP, CCP, CRP, ADP, SDP, SAP), metrics I/A/D, zones

### Takeaway
The six principles were defined in 1996–97 and condensed in 2000. They were renamed from "package" to "component" (unit of deployment) in 2017. Three are cohesion principles that pull against each other, so packaging must "jitter and evolve" over a project's life. Three are coupling principles: the dependency graph must be a DAG, dependencies should point toward stability, and stability should match abstractness. The metrics (I, A, D) are definitional. Martin himself calls them imperfect and warns against unconditional conformance. CCP's justification moved from OCP closure (1996) to "SRP for components" (2017).

### Cited Findings
- **1996 statements** [primary; definition/argument; source: [Martin 1996, "Granularity", C++ Report (column 5); cited as [Granularity97] in the 2000 paper](https://cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/Granularity-1996.pdf)]:
  - REP: "THE GRANULE OF REUSE IS THE GRANULE OF RELEASE. ONLY COMPONENTS THAT ARE RELEASED THROUGH A TRACKING SYSTEM CAN BE EFFECTIVELY REUSED. THIS GRANULE IS THE PACKAGE."
  - CRP: "THE CLASSES IN A PACKAGE ARE REUSED TOGETHER. IF YOU REUSE ONE OF THE CLASSES IN A PACKAGE, YOU REUSE THEM ALL."
  - CCP: "THE CLASSES IN A PACKAGE SHOULD BE CLOSED TOGETHER AGAINST THE SAME KINDS OF CHANGES. A CHANGE THAT AFFECTS A PACKAGE AFFECTS ALL THE CLASSES IN THAT PACKAGE." It is justified as "'closure' in the OCP sense" ("Closure must be strategic") and by "More important than reusability, is maintainability."
  - ADP: "THE DEPENDENCY STRUCTURE BETWEEN PACKAGES MUST BE A DIRECTED ACYCLIC GRAPH (DAG)." Cycles "make it very difficult to isolate modules. Unit testing and releasing become very difficult".
- **Breaking cycles (1996)** [primary]: "1. Apply the Dependency Inversion Principle (DIP)... 2. Create a new package that both MyDialogs and MyApplication depend upon. Move the class(es) that they both depend upon into that new package." "as the application grows, the package dependency structure jitters and grows." — [Granularity](https://cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/Granularity-1996.pdf)
- **Bottom-up structure (1996)** [primary]: "The package structure cannot be designed from the top down." "package dependency diagrams have very little do to with describing the function of the application. Instead, they are a map of how to build the application." "the package dependency structure grows and evolves with the logical design of the system." — [Granularity](https://cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/Granularity-1996.pdf). Echoed in 2017: "Instead, they are a map to the buildability and maintainability of the application." [secondary: highlight] — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/94952634-di-fan)
- **1997: stability, SDP, SAP and the metrics** [primary; source: [Stability](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf)]:
  - Stability: "stability is not a measure of the likelihood that a module will change; rather it is a measure of the difficulty in changing a module."
  - SDP: "A PACKAGE SHOULD ONLY DEPEND UPON PACKAGES THAT ARE MORE STABLE THAT IT IS." CCP is what creates deliberately volatile packages.
  - Metrics: Ca = "The number of classes outside this package that depend upon classes within this package". Ce = "The number of classes inside this package that depend upon classes outside this package". I = Ce/(Ca+Ce). "I=0 indicates a maximally stable package."
  - "the I metric is easiest to calculate when you have organized your source code such that there is one class in each source file."
  - SAP: "PACKAGES THAT ARE MAXIMALLY STABLE SHOULD BE MAXIMALLY ABSTRACT. INSTABLE PACKAGES SHOULD BE CONCRETE. THE ABSTRACTION OF A PACKAGE SHOULD BE IN PROPORTION TO ITS STABILITY."
  - A = abstract classes / total classes ("This is not a perfect metric").
  - Main sequence from (0,1) to (1,0). D = |(A+I−1)/√2| on [0, ~0.707]; normalized D′ = |A+I−1|.
  - Zones: "(0,0) is a zone of exclusion" (e.g., "a database schema"), but a non-volatile concrete "string" package is "harmless in the (0,0) zone". "Packages in the (1,1) zone are pretty meaningless."
  - "in my experience only about half the packages in a project can have such ideal characteristics."
  - Caveats: "a metric is not a god; it is merely a measurement against an arbitrary standard". "I would deeply regret it if anybody suddenly decided that all their designs must unconditionally be conformant to 'The Martin Metrics'."
- **2000 condensation** [primary; source: [Martin 2000](http://staff.cs.utu.fi/~jounsmed/doos_06/material/DesignPrinciplesAndPatterns.pdf)]:
  - One-line statements:
    - REP: "The granule of reuse is the granule of release."
    - CCP: "Classes that change together, belong together."
    - CRP: "Classes that aren't reused together should not be grouped together."
    - ADP: "The dependencies betwen packages must not form cycles."
    - SDP: "Depend in the direction of stability."
    - SAP: "Stable packages should be abstract packages."
  - Tension: "These three principles are mutually exclusive. They cannot simultaneously be satisfied... The REP and CRP makes life easy for reusers, whereas the CCP makes life easier for maintainers." "The CCP strives to make packages as large as possible... The CRP, however, tries to make packages very small." "Early in a project ... CCP dominates ... Later ... maximize REP and CRP".
  - Cycles: "Cycles can be broken in two ways. The first involves creating a new package, and the second makes use of the DIP and ISP." "Interfaces are very often included in the package that uses them, rather than in the package that implements them."
  - "stability has nothing direclty to do with frequency of change". SDP restated: "Depend upon packages whose I metric is lower than yours." "the SAP is just a restatement of the DIP."
  - Names: "zone of uselessness" (1,1) and "zone of pain" (0,0).
  - Ce is redefined as "The number of classes outside the package that classes inside the package depend upon."
  - "They are imperfect, and reliance upon them as the sole indicator of a sturdy architecture would be foolhardy."
- **c. 2005 statements** [primary]: CCP "Classes that change together are packaged together." CRP "Classes that are used together are packaged together." ADP "The dependency graph of packages must have no cycles." SAP "Abstractness increases with stability." The first three are labelled package cohesion; the last three are labelled coupling, with metrics. — [Principles of OOD](http://butunclebob.com/ArticleS.UncleBob.PrinciplesOfOod)
- **2017 (components)** [secondary: verbatim highlights; source: [Goodreads (di fan)](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/94952634-di-fan), [Goodreads (O. Santana Neto)](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/29536501-osvaldo-santana-neto)]:
  - "Components are the units of deployment." "In Java, they are jar files."
  - CCP: "Gather into components those classes that change for the same reasons and at the same times."
  - CRP: "The CRP is the generic version of the ISP." "The CRP says that classes that are not tightly bound to each other should not be in the same component."
  - Tension: "A good architect finds a position in that tension triangle that meets the current concerns of the development team, but is also aware that those concerns will change over time." "early in the development of a project, the CCP is much more important than the REP, because develop-ability is more important than reuse." "the composition of the components will likely jitter and evolve with time as the focus of the project changes from develop-ability to reusability."
  - Metrics: Fan-in = "the number of classes outside this component that depend on classes within the component". Fan-out = "the number of classes inside this component that depend on classes outside the component". "I = Fan-out / (Fan-in + Fan-out)". "I metrics should decrease in the direction of dependency."
  - SAP: "A component should be as abstract as it is stable." "The SAP and the SDP combined amount to the DIP for components."
  - Zone of pain: "Nonvolatile components are harmless in the (0, 0) zone... it is only volatile software components that are problematic in the Zone of Pain."
  - Dynamic languages (ch. 10, ISP, about declarations that clients must import; placed by the highlight's ~22% book position): "In dynamically typed languages like Ruby and Python, such declarations don't exist in source code. Instead, they are inferred at runtime. Thus there are no source code dependencies to force recompilation and redeployment. This is the primary reason that dynamically typed languages create systems that are more flexible and less tightly coupled than statically typed languages."
- **2017 statements seen only in reader highlights or search excerpts** [secondary, weaker]: ADP as "Allow no cycles in the component dependency graph." and CRP as "Don't force users of a component to depend on things they don't need." — [search result pointing to ch. 14 and Goodreads](https://oreilly.com/library/view/clean-architecture-a/9780134494272/ch14.xhtml)
- **Definitions as codified by others:** D = |A + I − 1|. Wikipedia cites *Agile Software Development* (2002), p. 262, for the instability index. [secondary] — [Wikipedia, "Software package metrics"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_package_metrics)

### Inferences
- **How the formulations drifted.** CCP's rationale moved from OCP-closure plus release cost (1996) to "SRP for components" (2017). CRP went from positive ("reused together", 1996/2005) to negative and ISP-like (2000/2017). In 2000 the tension paired REP+CRP (reusers) against CCP (maintainers); in 2017 it became a triangle.
- **What stayed constant.** Martin consistently treats package or component structure as emergent and build-oriented, not a functional decomposition.
- **Ce is counted inconsistently.** It counts inside classes (1997 definition, 2017), outside classes (2000, and the 1997 worked example: "Ce=3" counts outside targets), or package-level edges in later tools. Anyone implementing I must pick one and document it.
- **The economic case depends on separate release.** The economics behind CCP/CRP/REP/ADP are separate release, revalidation and redeploy costs. These weaken for modules inside a single, jointly-released package.

### Gaps
- The edge labels of the 2017 tension diagram and its "inclusive/exclusive" wording (REP+CCP enlarge components, CRP shrinks them) were not verified verbatim.
- The exact 2017 REP text was not verified.
- The 1997 PDF's "÷√2" glyph was garbled in extraction. The formula was reconstructed from the stated range [0, ~0.707].

## 3. Clean Architecture's Dependency Rule, "level", policy vs detail

### Takeaway
"Level" in Martin's sense is semantic: distance from inputs and outputs. Dependencies must point toward higher-level policy, and the Dependency Inversion Principle (DIP) is used wherever control flow goes the other way. It is not a level computed from the import graph, although stable, depended-upon policy ends up at the bottom of the dependency DAG.

### Cited Findings
- **2012 blog.** "This rule says that source code dependencies can only point inwards. Nothing in an inner circle can know anything at all about something in an outer circle." "The outer circles are mechanisms. The inner circles are policies." "the circles are schematic. You may find that you need more than just these four." Boundaries are crossed with DIP and "dynamic polymorphism". "Typically the data that crosses the boundaries is simple data structures." [primary] — [Martin 2012, "The Clean Architecture", Clean Coder Blog, 13 Aug 2012](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html)
- **2017 Dependency Rule:** "Source code dependencies must point only inward, toward higher-level policies." [secondary: highlight] — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/94952634-di-fan)
- **2017 "Policy and Level" (ch. 19) and the following Business Rules chapter** [secondary: highlights; source: [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/29536501-osvaldo-santana-neto)]:
  - "A strict definition of 'level' is 'the distance from the inputs and outputs.'" "The farther a policy is from both the inputs and the outputs of the system, the higher its level. The policies that manage input and output are the lowest-level policies in the system."
  - "We want source code dependencies to be decoupled from data flow and coupled to level."
  - "Higher-level policies ... tend to change less frequently, and for more important reasons... Lower-level policies ... tend to change frequently, and with more urgency, but for less important reasons."
  - "lower-level components should be plugins to the higher-level components."
  - "Why are Entities high level and use cases lower level? Because use cases are specific to a single application and, therefore, are closer to the inputs and outputs".
- **1996 DIP as summarised in 1997:** "the modules that implement high level policy should not depend upon the modules that implement low level details. Rather both high level policy and low level details should depend upon abstractions." [primary] — [Stability](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf)

### Inferences
- Martin's "level" is assigned by meaning (policy vs I/O detail) and then enforced on dependencies. Highest-level policy sits innermost (most depended upon, I≈0). So "high level" in Martin's sense corresponds to the bottom of a dependency DAG, not to a high layer index.
- The level/change-rate link ties the Dependency Rule back to SDP. Higher-level policy is claimed to change less often, so depending on it is depending in the direction of stability. This is asserted from experience and is not tested.

### Gaps
- A recalled ch. 19 sentence (policies that change for the same reasons and at the same times are at the same level and belong in the same component) could not be verified verbatim.

## 4. Critiques and empirical evidence

### Takeaway
Critics (North, Henney, Cecconi) attack three things:
- the vagueness and non-operational nature of "reason to change";
- premature, artificial splitting;
- the framing of design as binary rules.

Martin's 2014/2017 actor reformulation is partly a response, but it makes SRP depend on organizational structure, which code cannot show. Empirical evidence is thin. No study isolates SRP or CCP. Studies of SOLID as a bundle and of Martin's package metrics are few, small or single-system, Java-centric and correlational, with modestly positive results.

### Cited Findings
- **Dan North, "Why Every Element of SOLID is Wrong"**, a "Five minute Ignite-style talk from PubConf London 2016" (deck posted 20 Jan 2017). The SRP slides read: "'one reason to change' 'only do one thing'"; "What is a single responsibility anyway? ETL: three responsibilities or one? How can you predict what is going to change? Pointlessly Vague Principle". His replacement: "Simple code is easy to reason about. Can easily do several related things. Refactor until it Fits In Your Head. Write simple code". [primary: deck transcript; opinion] — [North 2017, Speaker Deck](https://speakerdeck.com/tastapod/why-every-element-of-solid-is-wrong)
- **North, "CUPID — for joyful coding"**, 10 Feb 2022 [primary; opinion/experience; source: [North 2022](https://dannorth.net/blog/cupid-for-joyful-coding/)]:
  - "Principles are like rules: you are either compliant or you are not."
  - "'doing one thing well' is an outside-in perspective... SRP is an inside-out perspective: it is about the organization of code."
  - SRP "creates artificial seams, and the most common case is where the content and format of the data change together".
  - Splitting "may be a premature optimization preventing a more natural separation of concerns emerging as the codebase grows".
  - Footnote: SRP is "trivially easy to refute—on the basis that even a single line of code may need to change for reasons of security, compliance, upstream or downstream dependencies, operational characteristics etc." and is "often a premature segregation with negative consequences".
  - On Rails: separating view and controller code "increases cognitive load, reduces cohesion".
  - Note that North targets the "reason to change" wording and Wikipedia's report example, not the actor formulation.
- **Martin's reply (2020).** "Dan North's slides completely miss the point on this, and convinces me that he did not understand the principle at all. (or that he was being ironic...) His answer to the SRP is to 'Write Simple Code'. I agree. The SRP is one of the ways we keep the code simple." "Microservices do not solve this problem." [primary] — [Martin 2020](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2020/10/18/Solid-Relevance.html)
- **Kevlin Henney, "SOLID Deconstruction" (ACCU Conference 2012 slides).** The SRP slides set the Wikipedia definition and DeMarco-cohesion lineage against: cohesion as coherence; the Unix philosophy, with John D. Cook's "The hard part is combining little programs"; "unit bias"; and Booch's "Every class should embody only about 3–5 distinct responsibilities". The argument is implicit in the slides. [primary: slides; opinion] — [Henney 2012](https://accu.org/content/conf2012/Kevlin_SOLID_Deconstruction.pdf). A summary of a recorded Henney talk reports his view that SRP "should be _few_ rather than single, and is more about **cohesion**". [secondary] — [Dunn 2021, "Is SOLID still relevant?", 19 Apr 2021](https://dunnhq.com/posts/2021/solid-relevance/)
- **Marco Cecconi, 6 May 2014**, two days before Martin's post that answers the bug-fix question. Cecconi argues that people cannot "unequivocally agree" on what a reason to change is ("Is a bug a valid reason to change?"), that SRP "only applies retroactively", and that "I could say 'three' reasons and be just as arbitrary". He adds: "All the examples I see are one-way towards simply creating a million single method classes." He proposes sizing classes by coupling and cohesion instead. [secondary: page summary with quotes; opinion] — [Cecconi 2014](https://sklivvz.com/posts/i-dont-love-the-single-responsibility-principle)
- **Martin's own limits.** See §1 ("no symptom" means do not apply; "Needless Complexity") and §2 ("a metric is not a god"; "only about half the packages"). [primary]
- **Validity of Martin's metric.** "there is lack of theoretical and empirical evaluation of the Martin's metric." A theoretical validation "reveals a weakness in Martin's definition for cohesion". The paper also includes an experimental evaluation on five OSS applications. [secondary: abstract; argument + empirical, n=5 apps] — [Hyrynsalmi & Leppänen 2009, "A Validation of Martin's Metric", DOI 10.13140/2.1.3645.3448](https://doi.org/10.13140/2.1.3645.3448)
- **Fault prediction.** Packages in Eclipse, pre- and post-release faults: "the prediction models that are based on Martin suite are more accurate than those that are based on MOOD and CK suites" across releases. [secondary: abstract; empirical, single large Java system] — [Elish, Al-Yafei & Al-Mulhem 2011, *Advances in Engineering Software* 42(10):852–859](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advengsoft.2011.06.001)
- **Understandability.** Five package-level metrics (size, coupling, stability) against understanding effort, on 18 packages from two OSS systems: "statistically significant correlation between most of the metrics and understandability". [secondary: abstract; empirical, small n] — [Elish 2010, ICPC](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICPC.2010.43)
- **Responsibility-weighted metrics.** Modified I/A weighted by "responsibility". The same authors report "very promising" relations to maintainability and testability. [secondary: abstracts; empirical, author-validated] — [Almugrin et al. 2014, COMPSAC](https://doi.org/10.1109/COMPSAC.2014.59); [Almugrin & Melton 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSA.2015.25)
- **SOLID and comprehension.** A controlled experiment, "three independent trials involving 100 data scientists", compared original and SOLID-restructured industrial ML code. It found "statistically significant evidence that the adoption of the SOLID design principles can improve code understanding". The five principles are applied as a bundle. [secondary: abstract; empirical] — [Cabral et al. 2024, CAIN](https://doi.org/10.1145/3644815.3644957)
- **SOLID and code metrics.** Code with and without SOLID was compared using Visual Studio metrics mapped to ISO/IEC 9126 maintainability. These are metric outcomes, not human outcomes. [secondary: abstract] — [Turan & Tanrıöver 2018, AJIT-e](https://doi.org/10.5824/1309-1581.2018.4.001.x)
- **LLMs and SOLID detection.** Four LLMs were tested on 240 manually validated examples. "GPT-4o Mini decisively outperforming others, yet even it struggles with challenging principles like DIP". Accuracy "degrades sharply with increasing code complexity". [secondary: abstract; empirical] — [Pehlivan et al. 2025, ASE](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00350)

### Inferences
- **What the evidence covers.** The strongest support is for package-level coupling and stability metrics as fault and understandability correlates (Eclipse, small samples). There is no evidence on whether one-responsibility units, or the main-sequence target (D≈0), cause better outcomes.
- **North vs Martin.** Their dispute is largely about operationalization. Both endorse "change together, stay together" in some form: North via domain-based structure, Martin via CCP and actors.

## 5. How SRP is operationalized at module or package level

### Takeaway
There are three routes:
1. **Actor or domain judgment** (Martin's actors and "screaming architecture"; North's domain-based structure). This is human and organizational.
2. **Co-change history** (CCP: "change together"). This is empirical but needs history.
3. **Structural dependency metrics** (I/A/D, cycle detection). These are computable but measure stability, not responsibility.

### Cited Findings
- **Martin's 2010 operationalization.** Split Employee into business-rule, reporter and repository classes. "Each of these classes can be placed in a component of its own. Or rather, all the reporting classes can go into the reporting component." [primary] — [Martin 2010](https://github.com/97-things/97-things-every-programmer-should-know/blob/master/en/thing_76/README.md)
- **"Screaming architecture."** "When you look at the top-level directory structure, and the source files in the highest-level package, do they scream 'Health Care System,' or 'Accounting System,'..." Simon Brown's chapter: "'package by component' keeps the user interface separate from these coarse-grained components." [secondary: highlights] — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/94952634-di-fan)
- **North's domain-based structure.** "module boundaries become domain boundaries". [primary] — [North 2022](https://dannorth.net/blog/cupid-for-joyful-coding/)
- **Metric tooling from the start.** Martin distributed "a bash script that scans a C++ directory structure and calculates all these metrics". [primary] — [Stability](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf)
- **Co-change and coupling studies.** Across 16 OSS projects, the congruence of package modularization was compared with "structural dependencies, fan-out similarity, evolutionary coupling, code ownership, code clones, and semantic similarity". Results are not in the abstract. [secondary] — [Beck & Diehl 2011, ESEC/FSE](https://doi.org/10.1145/2025113.2025162). Co-change clusters have been used to assess modularity. [secondary: title only] — [Silva, Valente & Maia 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-46734-3_3)

### Inferences
- Only route 3 is statically checkable without history or human input.
- CCP is the only package principle that directly encodes "responsibility". Detecting violations needs version history: modules that always change together, or one change spread across many modules.

### Gaps
- Tools such as JDepend and NDepend, which implement Martin's metrics, were not verified in this session.
- No source was found on Python or Rust tools that operationalize SRP at module level.

## Relevance to ln-structure

**(a) What the sources support**
- **Strict acyclic module graph.** This is ADP ("must not form cycles"; cycles hinder isolation, unit testing and release) — [Martin 2000](http://staff.cs.utu.fi/~jounsmed/doos_06/material/DesignPrinciplesAndPatterns.pdf); [Granularity](https://cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/Granularity-1996.pdf). Allowing cycles among files inside one module matches ADP's scope, which is inter-package, not intra-package.
- **Computed, meaning-free layers.** Martin repeatedly says the package graph "cannot be designed from the top down", has "very little do to with describing the function", and is "a map of how to build" ([Granularity](https://cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/Granularity-1996.pdf)). Computing a layer as 1 + the highest dependency fits this bottom-up, build-map view.
- **One class per file.** Martin noted that I "is easiest to calculate" with "one class in each source file" ([Stability](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf)). Clean Architecture equates a module with a source file, though that wording was not verified (see §1 Gaps). So ln files sit at Martin's SRP level and ln module folders at his component (CCP/CRP) level.
- **Surfaces and surface-only imports.** These fit CRP/ISP: "A dependency upon a package is a dependency upon everything within the package" ([Martin 2000](http://staff.cs.utu.fi/~jounsmed/doos_06/material/DesignPrinciplesAndPatterns.pdf)).
- **Restricting dependency inversion to plugins, wired at the entry point.** This fits several Martin points: lower-level components should be "plugins"; "Nobody except main needs to know that it exists" ([SRP chapter](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/SRP-1996.pdf)); and the two cycle-breaking remedies, of which "create a new package" is ln's default.
- **Humans write the one-line responsibility.** This makes the human the arbiter of the "axis of change", which Martin and his critics agree cannot be inferred from code alone.

**(b) What the sources challenge or contradict**
- **ln's responsibility criterion is not Martin's.** ln's line says what a module "answers or owns", a content or ownership criterion. Martin's 2014/2017 SRP is about who requests changes (the actor). Martin's own Employee class "owns" one concept yet violates SRP because three actors drive it ([Martin 2014](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/08/SingleReponsibilityPrinciple.html)). A one-line "what it owns" statement can pass while Martin's SRP fails.
- **The level is upside-down relative to Martin.** Martin's "high level" (policy, most depended upon, I≈0) sits at ln's l0 end. ln's higher layers hold dependent detail and wiring. Documentation should not borrow Martin's "higher-level" wording.
- **Layering does not imply SDP, and SAP is ignored.** Example: module A in l2 with 10 dependents and 1 dependency (I≈0.09) may depend on B in l1 with 1 dependent and 1 dependency (I=0.5). That is legal in ln but violates SDP.
- **The abstractness metric fits ln poorly.** ln publishes mostly concrete types, and Python declares abstractions only optionally (ABCs/Protocols; my inference). So A and D would push stable low-layer modules into the "zone of pain" unless they are non-volatile, which Martin himself exempts ([Stability](http://web.archive.org/web/2011/http://www.objectmentor.com/resources/articles/stability.pdf)).
- **Mandatory single responsibility for every module conflicts with Martin's own conditional SRP** (apply it only when the change occurs, or the result is "Needless Complexity"; [SRP chapter](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/SRP-1996.pdf)). It also runs into North's "premature segregation" ([North 2022](https://dannorth.net/blog/cupid-for-joyful-coding/)), Cecconi's "million single method classes" ([Cecconi 2014](https://sklivvz.com/posts/i-dont-love-the-single-responsibility-principle)) and Henney's "few rather than single" ([Dunn 2021](https://dunnhq.com/posts/2021/solid-relevance/)). For agents, the risk is over-fragmentation into many tiny modules and deep layer chains.
- **Martin's packaging economics barely apply inside one package.** CCP, CRP and REP rest on separate release and redeploy costs. These weaken for folders inside one Python package or Rust crate; what remains is comprehension and change localization. For Python, Martin concedes in Clean Architecture's ISP chapter that there are "no source code dependencies to force recompilation and redeployment" (see §2).
- **ln has no notion of changing granularity over time.** Martin's tension triangle says granularity should "jitter and evolve" ([Goodreads highlights](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36239393-clean-architecture/94952634-di-fan)). ln has no guidance on merging modules that always co-change.

**(c) Open questions**
- Should the responsibility line also name the change driver (actor/"changes when...")?
- Should ff-lntools report I, D or SDP violations as advisories? If so, which Ce counting unit should it use, and how should A be defined for Python, or for Rust traits?
- Can co-change in agent-written git history detect CCP violations? Agent commit patterns may not reflect domain change.
- What evidence would show that computed depth predicts anything (defects, change propagation)? None was found.
- How reliably can LLM agents judge responsibility adherence, given limited LLM accuracy on SOLID violations ([Pehlivan et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00350))?
